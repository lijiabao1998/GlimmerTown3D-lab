## Battersea Power Station (k5)

Battersea, south bank of the River Thames, London, England

Surviving former power station; repurposed and open as a mixed-use destination. Do not describe the game power-generation role as its current real-world use.

Exterior cues: Massive reddish-brown brick rectilinear body with lower side wings; four widely spaced tall ivory cylindrical chimneys atop stepped brick corner wash towers; slender vertical chimney fluting and two narrow top rings; long narrow recessed window/pier strips; grey-blue glass roof additions. Preserve the four-chimney silhouette before adding small details.

## Jumbo Water Tower (Balkerne Water Tower) (k10)

Balkerne Passage, Colchester, Essex, England

Surviving Grade II* former municipal water tower. Opened 1883, decommissioned in the 1980s; current heritage project seeks sustainable reuse.

Exterior cues: Square red-brick tower with four massive corner piers and very tall round arches between them; central service core; two rows of small dark arcaded/bracket openings immediately below projecting rust-red square iron tank; green-patinated copper pyramidal roof, tiny square lantern/cupola and weather vane. Must read as masonry arcade supporting square tank, not a generic tank on thin metal legs.

## Natural History Museum, Waterhouse building (k35)

Cromwell Road, South Kensington, London, England

Surviving and active museum building. Official museum account identifies Alfred Waterhouse’s Romanesque design and terracotta decoration.

Exterior cues: Two tall square entrance towers flanking a lower central gabled facade and monumental concentric round-arch doorway; paired/triple Romanesque round-arch windows; alternating buff/grey-blue horizontal terracotta bands; steep dark pyramidal tower caps with corner pinnacles and finials; broad low side wings with repetitive arches and small gabled dormers. Pale warm terracotta rather than flat white stone.

- [Natural History Museum architecture account](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/alfred-waterhouse-museum-building-cathedral-to-nature.html)

## Royal Albert Hall (k36)

Kensington Gore, London SW7 2AP, England

Surviving Grade I concert and public performance hall. Existing game theatre category is an artistic/gameplay mapping, not a claim that it is a conventional drama theatre.

Exterior cues: Broad elliptical red-brick drum with cream/buff terracotta trim; lower rectangular windows, upper round-arch windows and closely spaced pilasters; continuous pale figurative frieze band near the top; shallow ribbed glass dome over a narrower red upper drum; projecting entrance block/porch with triangular pediment. Use decorative blocks for the frieze, not the source image.

## Radcliffe Camera (k41)

Radcliffe Square, Oxford OX1 3BG, England

Surviving working library within the central Bodleian Library complex; not Oxford’s municipal central library. Built to James Gibbs’s design and opened 1749.

Exterior cues: Honey-coloured circular rotunda with rough rusticated base and large round arches; tall upper ring of paired classical columns, dark rectangular windows and blind niches; heavy projecting cornice and balustrade with urns; smaller grey lead-covered segmented dome with circular dormers, open lantern and gold top finial. Dome is substantially narrower than lower drum.

## Palm House, Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew (k63)

Kew Gardens, Richmond upon Thames, London, England

Surviving Grade I Victorian glasshouse, opened 1848; official page says it remains open ahead of a planned 2027 renovation. This is an exterior study of the familiar building, not a representation of its changing plant collection or works.

Exterior cues: White/cream fine iron ribs and dense glazing grid over green-grey transparent glass; elongated curving roof form resembling an overturned ship; tall central curved roof over lower glazed portions; raised linear roof clerestory; tall white-framed semicircular entrance bay and low stone plinth. Use repeated curved ribs with tinted procedural glazing, not an opaque green dome. The inspected photo shows central portion, not entire long-wing extent.

## Elizabeth Tower (k67)

Northeast corner of the Palace of Westminster, London, England

Surviving restored clock tower. Big Ben is the principal bell, although commonly used as a nickname for the tower. Four clock faces are a key architectural feature.

Exterior cues: Very slender honey-stone square shaft; many horizontal floor divisions and narrow dark vertical openings; four huge white circular dials with Prussian-blue frames, hands and Roman-numeral marks inside gold-trimmed square panels; pointed-arch belfry over clocks; steep dark slate roof in two tiers, separated by open gold-accented lantern stage; gold finial and sparse gold roof details.

## Smeaton's Tower (k69)

The Hoe, Plymouth PL1 2NZ, Devon, England

Surviving relocated upper section of the former Eddystone Lighthouse, now a historic visitor landmark. About two thirds of the 1759 structure was moved to Plymouth Hoe in the 1880s. It is not the current operational offshore Eddystone lighthouse.

Exterior cues: Tapering circular painted masonry tower, flaring toward the foot; five wide body bands bottom to top red-white-red-white-red; small vertically staggered windows; pale projecting circular balcony and railing; octagonal glazed lantern with fine bars; pale ogee-shaped cap, gold ball and weather vane. Keep the red base, white roof and glass lantern recognizable.
